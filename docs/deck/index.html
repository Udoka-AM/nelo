The problem

A quarter of card payments in this market simply fail.

Adaeze sells phone accessories. Her bank terminal took a deposit, a wait, and a fee she never stops noticing — and it stops working whenever the network drops, which is most afternoons.

On those afternoons she takes cash, or she loses the sale.

This is one worked example of a condition that recurs wherever card acceptance is expensive and authorisation networks are unreliable — Lagos, Buenos Aires, Manila, Karachi, Lima.

A card machine with no card machine.

How it works

Offline mode is a prepaid balance, not a promise to pay.

01Lock

Payer locks funds into an on-chain vault while online. You cannot spend offline what you have not already locked.

02Sign

StrongBox signs a 202-byte voucher with a P-256 key that provably lives in secure hardware.

03Hand over

NFC or QR at version 10. Neither phone has a network. The merchant verifies the signature locally.

04Adjudicate

On reconnect the program checks the signature via the precompile, then a 128-slot replay window.

05Settle

USDC moves. A licensed partner converts and pays the merchant's bank in local currency.

  • Solvency is an on-chain fact before the sale, not a hope after it.
  • Replay is impossible — a repeated sequence is rejected deterministically, for everyone, identically.
  • Withdrawal is timelocked so nobody can spend offline then race home to empty the vault.
  • Residual risk is insured — the same way card schemes have priced floor limits for fifty years.

Why a phone, why Solana

Neither half of this works anywhere else.

Only on a phone

The claim requires a secure element and a short-range radio in the same device at the same instant. There is no laptop version, no web version, and nothing that can be retrofitted to a transaction after the fact.

The rules forbid "a website in a wrapper". Nelo could not be one if it tried.

Only on Solana

The secp256r1 precompile is what lets the chain verify a secure-element signature directly. Sub-cent fees and sub-second finality are what make a $3 sale economic. Remove either and the design collapses back into trusting a server.

Solana carries roughly 35% of global on-chain stablecoin transfers by count.

SKR integration — the $10,000 specialist award

The rebate is not a reward. It is collateral the merchant is paid to post.

Nelo carries offline risk. A merchant who stakes is underwriting part of their own exposure, which reduces the reserve Nelo must hold. They are compensated for taking risk the platform would otherwise carry — a deductible, not a bribe.

  • Funded from revenue, never minted. 20% of the platform fee buys SKR on the open market. Zero volume, zero cost.
  • It arrives already staked — an accrual of collateral, not a payout.
  • It raises the offline limit on a sublinear, reputation-weighted, hard-capped curve. Trust cannot simply be bought.
  • It is first-loss capital. The stake absorbs a conflict before the platform reserve does.
  • It earns while it sits — delegated to a Guardian, so risk capital is productive.
  • Unstaking waits out the settlement horizon, so nobody can unstake to escape a pending loss.
Remove SKR and the risk model gets strictly worse

The floor limit has nothing to key off, and the reserve must grow. That is the difference between an integration and a sticker — and it uses SKR's actual designed utility, staking and Guardian delegation, rather than inventing a parallel one.

Distribution

Fifty merchants on foot, then let the network do the rest.

  • Cluster by cluster. Win one row of stalls completely before opening a second city. Merchants in a market talk constantly — density beats reach.
  • Peer transfer as the spreading agent. Every customer who pays offline has the app, and every one of them can pay a friend.
  • The dApp Store placement a winner receives seeds the customer side with users who already hold wallets.
  • The Trust Stake is a retention loop. A merchant with staked collateral and a raised limit has a structural reason to keep volume here.
  • Payout partners become a channel. Disbursement providers already have merchant relationships and an interest in volume — the integration that gates you at the start distributes you later.
The first hundred are doorstep work

Not marketing. It is also the product research — you learn more from the tenth merchant refusing than from any amount of design.

The one blocking dependency

A licensed disbursement partner in the launch corridor. Everything else in the stack is open or self-serve. If that agreement does not land, the product is a demo with a stubbed settlement leg — which is why it is chased on day one, not month two.

Team

Who built it, and what unlocks the next stage.

Fill — team

Four roles: Android/Kotlin for the secure element and NFC; Rust/Anchor for the vault and the trust model; design, because UX is a quarter of the score and a POS is where crypto apps look worst; and someone commercial to unblock the payout partner.

  • An introduction to a licensed disbursement partner in one emerging market.
  • A design partner — one market association or merchant cluster willing to run fifty terminals.
  • A security review of the vault program before real money moves through it.

Everything else — the chain, the secure element, the standard — already exists. What is missing is a team willing to write Android-native code against a payments risk model, and one commercial relationship.
